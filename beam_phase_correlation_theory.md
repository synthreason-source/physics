# Beam‑Phase Correlation Theory  
*(Interpretive layer on standard quantum optics and Bell nonlocality)*

> **Scope:** This document presents a conceptual interpretation of beam splitters, entanglement, and Bell experiments. The core physics follows standard quantum mechanics; the narrative framing ("phase‑coherent whole", "correlation residue", etc.) is an interpretive overlay, not a new dynamical theory. [1][2][4][5][6][12][14]

---

## Axioms (interpretive form)

### Axiom 1: Single‑amplitude input  
An incident photon is described by **one coherent quantum amplitude** before measurement, not by two independent physical halves. [1][9][14]

### Axiom 2: Beam‑splitter unitarity  
A beam splitter acts as a **unitary transformation** that maps one input amplitude into a superposition of output amplitudes with fixed relative probabilities and phases. [1][4][8][14]

### Axiom 3: Phase carries structure  
The physically relevant content of the splitter is not material subdivision but **phase‑structured redistribution of possibility** across output modes. [4][8][14]

### Axiom 4: Joint‑state primacy  
For paired or correlated systems, the **joint quantum state is primary**, and the observable outcomes of each arm are not fully defined independently of that joint state. [2][3][6][12]

### Axiom 5: Measurement conditioning  
Measurement on one subsystem **updates the conditional state assignment** of the other subsystem, but does **not** transmit a usable classical signal between them. [5][6][12]

---

## Definitions

### Definition 1: Beam‑phase mapping  
The transformation from one input mode to two output modes, including amplitudes and relative phase shifts, is called the **beam‑phase mapping**. [4][8][14]

### Definition 2: Correlation residue  
The part of the observed joint statistics that **cannot be explained** by assigning local pre‑existing values to each arm is called the **correlation residue**. [2][3][6][12][13]

### Definition 3: Non‑separable state  
A state is **non‑separable** when it cannot be written as a product of independent states for each subsystem. [2][6][10][12]

### Definition 4: Conditional collapse  
The updated description of one subsystem after measurement of the other is called **conditional collapse**. [5][6][12]

### Definition 5: Phase‑coherent whole  
A system whose observables are governed by a **single global phase structure** rather than independent local parts is called a **phase‑coherent whole**. [1][2][4][12]

## Core postulates (physics + interpretation)

- A beam splitter does **not** split photon substance. [1][9][14]  
- It **redistributes quantum possibility** via a unitary, phase‑structured map. [4][8][14]  
- The output probabilities are constrained by **unitary phase relations**. [1][4][8][14]  
- Bell‑type violations arise when the joint statistics of the outputs **cannot be reduced** to local assignments. [2][3][6][12][13]  
- Measurement reveals **one branch of a global state** and updates the conditional description of the partner subsystem, without enabling faster‑than‑light signaling. [5][6][12]

---

## Derived claims (aligned with standard quantum mechanics)

- The "50/50" behavior of an ideal beam splitter is an **amplitude symmetry**, not an energy division into two half‑photons. [1][9][14]  
- Entanglement is a **global constraint** on the joint state, not a local energetic disturbance. [2][6][10][12]  
- The apparent instantaneity of distant measurement correlation reflects the **structure of the state update**, not a propagating physical shock or signal. [5][6][12]  
- The role of the beam splitter in Bell‑type experiments is to **preserve or reveal phase relations** that classical hidden‑variable models cannot reproduce. [2][4][6][12]

## Predicted experimental consequences (standard QM expectations)

1. **Phase‑sensitive Bell visibility**  
   Changing the beam‑phase mapping by controlled phase shifts should change Bell‑type correlation visibility even when count rates remain fixed. [2][6][10]

2. **Thickness is not the controlling variable**  
   Increasing the physical thickness of the splitter alone should not determine whether the quantum balance holds, provided the optical transformation and phase relations are unchanged. [4][8][14]

3. **Correlation survives without local energy split**  
   Interference‑based Bell correlations persist even when no interpretation in terms of literal photon division is possible. [1][2][6][12]

4. **Conditional statistics shift after remote measurement**  
   Measuring one arm changes the **conditional statistics** of the partner arm, while the **marginal statistics** of the partner alone remain unchanged. [5][6][12]

5. **Phase‑breaking destroys nonclassical structure**  
   Introducing decoherence, distinguishability, or uncontrolled phase noise reduces the correlation residue and weakens Bell‑type violation. [2][6][12]

6. **Beam‑splitter asymmetry deforms correlations predictably**  
   A non‑50/50 splitter changes output probabilities consistently with unitary amplitude redistribution, while the nonclassical joint‑state structure remains analyzable via the modified transformation. [1][8][10][14]

## Interpretation of Bell experiments in this theory

Bell experiments are not about a wavelength literally splitting into two equal wavelengths. They test whether the observed joint outcomes can be reproduced by **local pre‑assigned values**. [2][3][6][12][13]

In this mini‑theory, Bell violation is evidence that the system is governed by a **shared phase‑coherent whole** whose outcomes are only locally revealed at measurement. [2][6][12]

---

## Theorem (concise statement)

> **Theorem:**  
> A beam splitter preserves a single quantum amplitude while distributing its possible outcomes across multiple paths by unitary phase mapping. If two such outputs are part of a non‑separable joint state, then their observed correlations may violate Bell inequalities because the statistics belong to the **global phase‑coherent whole** rather than to independent local properties. [1][2][4][6][12][14]

---

## Falsifiability conditions

This mini‑theory fails if any of the following are found:

- Bell‑type correlations can be fully explained by a purely **local model** with pre‑existing values and no shared phase structure. [2][3][6][12][13]  
- Beam‑splitter output statistics depend primarily on **thickness** rather than on the unitary optical transformation. [4][8][14]  
- Conditional state changes on one side can be used to transmit **controllable information instantly** to the other side (violating no‑signaling). [5][6][12]  
- Phase shifts and indistinguishability do **not** affect interference and Bell visibility as predicted. [2][6][10][12]

These may be interesting as **philosophical or science‑fictional extensions**, but they are not consequences of the core beam‑phase / Bell framework as understood in current quantum optics and quantum information theory. [5][6][11][12]

## Final compressed form

Reality at the quantum level is not built from independently split particles, but from **globally constrained amplitudes**. A beam splitter reveals **phase‑coherent redistribution of possibility**, and Bell violations reveal that the measurable world cannot be reduced to local pre‑assigned parts. [1][2][4][6][12][14]

All operational communication and causal influence remain limited by relativistic causality; entanglement provides **nonlocal correlations without signaling**. [5][6][12]
